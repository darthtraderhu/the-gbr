Plenty of business owners have run this test. They type into ChatGPT: *"Who should I buy industrial refrigeration from near Vienna?"*, and check whether they're in the answer.

If they are, they relax. If they aren't, they start to worry.

**Neither reaction is justified. One question, asked once, is not a measurement.** The same question tomorrow, on a different phone or in a different AI assistant, can give a different answer.

## Why the answer changes

An AI assistant doesn't read its answer out of a database. It writes it fresh every time, and several things feed into that.

**There's randomness built in.** Two consecutive runs of the same question can list different companies, or the same ones in a different order.

**It matters whether it searches the web.** If it does, it works from fresh results. If it doesn't, it works from what it knew when it was trained. There can be months between the two.

**It matters who's asking.** If the platform remembers your earlier conversations or knows where you are, the answer adapts to you. If you've asked about your own company before, that may be exactly why it comes up.

**And the platform matters.** ChatGPT, Gemini, Perplexity, Microsoft Copilot and Claude work from different sources under different rules. Google's AI Overview is built on Google's own results. What shows up in one won't necessarily show up in another.

## What you ask matters

The most common mistake is running the test with the company's name: *"What do you know about [your company]?"*

The AI will very likely say something, because you've just told it your name. **Your buyer doesn't ask that way.** They don't know you yet. They ask who to buy from, what to look out for, what it costs, who provides servicing.

So a useful test has two parts:

1. **Category questions**, the kind a buyer asks before they know anyone. The question here is whether you come up at all.
2. **Name questions**, the kind someone asks once they've heard of you and want to check. The question here is whether everything it says about you is accurate.

## Being wrong is worse than being missing

Most people only check whether they're in the answer. There's a more uncomfortable case: **you're in it, but described wrongly.**

An old address. A service you stopped offering years ago. A wrong phone number. A product range you never sold. A competitor's details next to your name.

The problem then isn't that buyers can't find you. It's that they find you, and form the wrong impression before they ever call.

**The only way to catch this is to read the answer word for word**, not just scan it for your company name.

## How to ask if you test it yourself

If you want a picture of your own, this is the minimum:

1. **Write down five to ten questions** the way a buyer would ask them. Include both category and name questions.
2. **Ask each question at least three times**, each time in a new conversation with no history. Ideally not from your own, everyday account.
3. **Try several platforms**, not just the one you use. Your buyers may not use the same one.
4. **Save the answers verbatim**, with the date and the platform. Six months from now, that's how you'll see whether anything changed.
5. **Note separately**: were you mentioned, was what you do described accurately, and who was mentioned instead of you.

That's a few hours of work. It isn't complicated, it just takes discipline.

## What to do with the results

If you're missing from the answers, or described inaccurately, the fix is almost never on the AI platform. **It's on your own site, and everywhere your company's details appear.**

Does your homepage say, in text, what you do and for whom? Are the address and phone number the same on the site, on your Google Business Profile and on social media? Is there machine-readable company data on the site? Does the site let AI assistants' crawlers in?

We've written about these in detail: [What does ChatGPT see on your site?](/en/writing/what-chatgpt-sees-on-your-site)

After the fixes, **run the same question set again.** That's the only way to tell whether the change shows up in the answers.

## What nobody can promise

Nobody can guarantee that an AI assistant will recommend you. The answers aren't written by the site's owner, or by whoever builds the site.
