Most companies start thinking about a new website when the old one has become embarrassing to show. The design is dated, it's slow, it's "not modern".

That's a fair feeling, but a poor starting point. **If you don't know where your site loses buyers today, the new one gets built blind too.** It will look better, and buyers will get stuck in the same place.

That's why we always start with an audit. The examples below come from audits we did in recent months, without company names.

## What doesn't come down to design

Most of the costliest problems don't show up in a screenshot.

**The shop's search returned nothing for its own example query.** The search box had a grey example of what to type. Anyone who typed it was told there was no such product. There was.

**A four-field quote request, then nothing.** No confirmation, no expected response time. The warmest lead doesn't know whether their request arrived, and meanwhile they write to a competitor too.

**On mobile, the price sat about 1,400 pixels below the product image.** Three screens of scrolling before the buyer gets an answer to their first question.

**Every subpage's canonical tag pointed to the home page.** It's an invisible setting that tells Google the site really has only one page. The product pages can't compete in search at all.

A new design fixes none of these on its own. If nobody names the problem, it can easily move over to the new site too.

## What a score won't tell you

Many people start with a free testing tool. They get a number, red and green rows, and a long list of technical terms.

That's a useful starting point, but **a score doesn't know what you sell, or to whom.** It doesn't know that a competitor's name appears twice on your home page because someone copied the text from there. It doesn't know there are three different sets of opening hours online. And it can't tell you which five of the forty red rows actually cost you money.

So a good audit answers three questions:

- **what is losing buyers today,** and why,
- **what will bring in money** once it's fixed,
- **what can wait,** because it's awkward but not urgent.

## And what you don't need to throw away

This question gets asked least, yet it's where you can save the most.

Almost every site has something that already works well. A well-structured price list. Real references with sizes and dates. A small in-house tool that customers actually use. A Google profile with plenty of good reviews.

**These don't need rebuilding; they need keeping and showing better.** And sometimes it turns out you don't need a new site at all, and the most important problems can be fixed on the current system.

## Why from the outside

We don't ask for access to do an audit. That's not about convenience.

**Your buyer can't see the admin panel either.** They see what's visible from the outside: on an average phone, on the move, arriving from a Google results page. Google and AI search tools see the same. If that's what we look at, we measure what actually shapes the decision.
