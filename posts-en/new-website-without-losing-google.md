The worst news after a new website launches isn't that someone dislikes it. It's that a few weeks later enquiries drop, and nobody knows why.

Often the reason is that **Google knew the old site's addresses, and those addresses are gone.** The old search results lead to a page that no longer exists, and over time Google removes them. The new site starts from zero, however good it looks.

This is the most expensive mistake in a platform change. It's also the easiest one to prevent.

## Rankings belong to the address, not the design

When someone searches for your product, Google doesn't show your company. It shows a specific page, for example `/products/industrial-filter-200`. Over the years, everything Google uses to judge how good a result is has built up on that address.

A new system often structures addresses differently. The old `/products/industrial-filter-200` becomes `/shop/filters/200-industrial-filter`. To you it's the same page. **To Google it's a new, unknown page, and the old one has vanished.**

The same happens to everything that points to the old addresses:

- Google search results,
- links on partners' sites, directories, old press coverage,
- old newsletters and bookmarks,
- and QR codes on printed material.

## What it takes not to lose it

It isn't complicated, but it has to be done in full, and before launch.

**1. An inventory of the old addresses.** Before anything changes, we collect every address of the old site: from the sitemap, from Search Console and by crawling the site. Especially the ones that bring traffic today.

**2. A redirect map.** Every old address gets a counterpart on the new site and a permanent (301) redirect. Whoever arrives at the old address, a person or Google, lands on the new one automatically, and Google knows to show the new page from now on. **Not to the home page, but to the closest matching page.** If every old product page leads to the home page, the buyer can't find what they were looking for, and Google can't tell what happened to the product.

**3. The old site doesn't go offline first.** It stays up until the new one demonstrably works. That's why we rebuild live stores in phases: pages are replaced step by step while the shop keeps trading.

**4. Monitoring after launch.** We submit the new sitemap to Search Console and watch for weeks where Google reports errors. Anything the redirects missed shows up here.

## The invisible settings move too

Besides the addresses, there are things that don't show on the page but matter a lot to search engines: page descriptions, structured data, canonical tags. These have to be carried over, or rebuilt properly.

We regularly find problems here in audits. **On one site, every page's canonical tag pointed to the home page.** That tells Google the site really has only one page, so the product pages can't compete at all. **On another, the sitemap had 16,519 entries, of which 863 were unique.** On a new site, this can be avoided from day one if someone is paying attention.
