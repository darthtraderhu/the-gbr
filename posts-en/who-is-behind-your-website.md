Before placing an order with a new supplier, a serious buyer checks who they are dealing with. They look up the company name, the registered address, the VAT number, and use the registration number to check in a company register that the company exists and who runs it.

**The first place they look is your website.** The footer, the legal notice, the contact page. If it isn't there, they won't email you to ask. They'll move on to the next quote, where they can find it.

Over the past months, several company websites we reviewed were missing these basics or had them wrong.

## What we found on the sites we reviewed

**No legal notice, no address, no tax number.** On one service company's site, four forms asked visitors for their name, phone number, address and even a photo of the fault. Nowhere could you find out which company you were sending all that to.

**A privacy page, but empty.** On the same site, the privacy page showed an unfilled template asking the owner to complete it in the admin panel. That is what visitors saw.

**Every footer link leads to an error page.** At one manufacturer we reviewed, twelve out of twelve footer links ended on "page not found", the privacy notice among them. There was no legal notice, and the phone number and email address were plain text: on a phone you couldn't tap them.

**The cookie notice carries another site's name.** A leftover from the template, as we wrote in [our article on pages you don't know about](/en/writing/pages-you-dont-know-about). To visitors it says one thing: nobody read this before it went live.

None of these is a dramatic failure. None of them takes the site down. **They just hurt with exactly the visitor who is already serious**, and wants to check who they are about to pay.

## What the law expects

In the EU, the E-Commerce Directive requires anyone providing an online service for business to make their basic details easily, directly and permanently accessible. National laws implement it (in Hungary, the act known as Ekertv.). The list includes, among others:

- the company's **name, geographic address** and contact details, including an **email address**,
- the **trade register** and registration number,
- the **VAT number**,
- details of the supervisory authority or professional body, where the activity requires authorisation or membership.

Hungarian law also asks for the **hosting provider's** name, address and email. If your site collects data, for example through a form, you also need a privacy notice that describes what really happens. We wrote about that [in a separate article](/en/writing/privacy-notice-that-describes-another-site).

This is not legal advice. Your lawyer will tell you exactly what applies to your company. **But if none of the details above are on your site, check today**, not after a buyer or a regulator asks.

## It's not only buyers: machines look too

When someone asks an AI assistant who a company is, where it is and what it does, the answer is built from whatever the machine can find. **If your own site has no clear company details, it takes them from elsewhere:** an old register entry, an outdated directory, an old map listing.

So it isn't enough for the details to sit somewhere in an image or a PDF. They need to be text, consistently in the footer, the legal notice and the structured data written for machines. We covered what machines see of your site in more detail [in this article](/en/writing/what-chatgpt-sees-on-your-site).

## Check it yourself in ten minutes

**Open your homepage on your phone and scroll to the footer.** Is the full company name there? Can you tap the phone number and the email address?

**Click every footer link.** Does each one lead to a real page with real content? Especially the legal notice, the privacy notice and your terms, if you have them.

**Find your VAT number and registration number.** If you can't find them on your own site within a minute, neither can your buyer.

**Read your legal pages like a stranger would.** Is it your company in there? Your address, your name, your hosting provider? No blanks, no other site's name?

**Fill in your own form.** Does it say who receives the data and why? And does the email arrive where it should?
